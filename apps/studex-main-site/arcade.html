Who is behind it

Ten years of building, in public

Studex started in 2016 with a blockchain platform that let people own a share of a farm animal in South Africa. The business grew into a group: premium halaal Wagyu and Ankole beef, speciality coffee, wheat, commodity trade across borders, and the cloud and agent platform that now runs all of it.

It is operated by a founder and a fleet of agents rather than a large head office. Every vertical is a test of the same idea: if a business is really a computer, then the work of running one can be written down, handed to agents, and watched on a screen. The ones that survive that test become products other companies can buy.

Season 01 footprint

Five countries, one pattern

Each country gets the same three pieces in the same order. A hub: a room with machines where people learn AI and code, mentors hold sessions, startups work and the Arcade runs its nights. An Agentic Rise cohort behind it, so the ones who are serious get a team of agents and a real brief. And compute close enough that the work actually runs. The hub is cheap and fast. The compute is the part that needs a partner.

A hub runs on three things: machines and connectivity from our infrastructure partners, the StudBot harness that puts an agent in front of every member, and models hosted close to the room — local where the hardware allows it, cloud where it doesn't. Nobody in the hub needs a foreign credit card to start working.

Rwanda's data-protection law requires personal data to be stored in-country unless the regulator authorises otherwise, and the grid has a dry-season shortfall that any node has to plan around. Those two facts shape the Kigali design more than anything else on this page.
