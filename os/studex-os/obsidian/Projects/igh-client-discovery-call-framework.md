# IGH Client Discovery Call Framework
## StudeX Super Agents — How to Run a Diagnostic Client Call

---

## Philosophy: Diagnose, Don't Prescribe

**Before the call:** The client is the expert on their business. We are the experts on AI-powered operations. Our job is to **listen deeply, ask precisely, and connect their pain to our solution** — without jumping to the product.

**During the call:** We are not selling. We are diagnosing. We ask questions that help the client see their own problems more clearly. We reflect back what we hear. We create urgency through understanding, not fear.

**After the call:** We send a personalised diagnosis + proposal within 24 hours.

## The Call Structure: 45 Minutes Total

```
[0-2 min]  WARM OPEN
           ↓
[2-10 min] PROBLEM DISCOVERY — "Walk me through your typical day"
           ↓
[10-20 min] TOOL AUDIT — "Show me what you're using right now"
           ↓
[20-28 min] PAIN DEEP DIVE — "Tell me more about that"
           ↓
[28-38 min] VISION EXTRACTION — "If you had one magic wand..."
           ↓
[38-43 min] CLOSE — Show demo, book next step
           ↓
[43-45 min] NEXT STEPS — Send diagnosis + proposal in 24h
```

## Section 1: Warm Open (0–2 minutes)

**Goal:** Build rapport. Make them feel heard before you ask anything hard.

**Script:**
> "Dr. Amelia — thank you so much for making time. I know your schedule is full with sessions all day, so I want to make these 45 minutes really count for you.
>
> Before we dive in, I want to be direct: we're not here to pitch you anything. Our job today is to understand exactly how Infinite Growth Haven runs — so we can show you where you're leaving money on the table and where AI can genuinely give you your time back.
>
> Does that sound good?"

**Key move:** Establish that you're a *diagnostic* partner, not a salesperson. This unlocks honesty.

---

## Section 2: Problem Discovery (2–10 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand their day-to-day. Find the operational bottlenecks. Quantify the chaos.

### Question 1: "Walk me through a typical day at IGH."
> *"From when you wake up to when you close the clinic — what does a normal day actually look like?"*

**What to listen for:**
- How many steps between a client enquiry and a confirmed booking
- Whether bookings happen in WhatsApp, phone, or in-person
- How many interruptions they face during sessions
- Whether they have any visibility into tomorrow's/wnext week's bookings
- How they handle enquiries after hours (while sleeping, while in a session)

**Follow-up if vague:** *"Can you walk me through one specific client journey — from the moment they first reach out to you, to the moment they walk out after their session?"*

### Question 2: "What part of your day frustrates you the most?"
> *"What takes up the most time that you feel shouldn't have to?"*

**What to listen for:**
- Manual WhatsApp responses (especially after hours)
- No-shows and last-minute cancellations
- Double-bookings or scheduling conflicts
- Chasing payments
- Forgetting to follow up with clients
- Managing multiple locations

---

## Section 3: Tool Audit (10–20 minutes)

**Goal:** Map every tool they currently use. Identify the patchwork. Find the gaps.

### Question 3: "Show me your current setup — what tools do you use to run IGH?"

**Tool categories to cover:**

| Category | Listen For |
|---|---|
| Booking | WhatsApp DMs, phone calls, physical diary, Google Calendar, none |
| Payments | WhatsApp transfer, EFT, POS machine, none, cash only |
| Client records | WhatsApp contacts, paper files, Excel, Google Sheets, none |
| Scheduling | WhatsApp check-ins, Google Calendar, manual |
| Social media | TikTok, Instagram, Facebook — who manages it? |
| Marketing | None, word of mouth, paid ads |
| Communication | WhatsApp, email, phone |
| Products/shop | None, WhatsApp orders, physical counter |
| Team | Do they have staff? Who does what? |

**Follow-up:** *"What's the most important piece of client information you wish you had right now that you don't have easy access to?"*

**RED FLAG (write this down):** If they say "I just use WhatsApp for everything" — that's a goldmine. Every WhatsApp-only operation is hemorrhaging leads.

---

## Section 4: Pain Deep Dive (20–28 minutes)

**Goal:** Quantify the pain. Get specific numbers. Create urgency.

### Question 4: "How many enquiries do you get per week — and how many of those become bookings?"

> *"Rough estimate is fine. If you had to guess, out of every 10 people who reach out to you, how many actually book?"*

**What to calculate:**
- **Enquiry volume:** X per week
- **Conversion rate:** Y%
- **Average booking value:** R550–R3,500 depending on service
- **Leakage:** (10 - Y)% × X × avg value = R___ per week lost

**Write it on screen:** "Based on what you've told me — you're losing approximately **R___ per week** in potential revenue. That's **R___ per month.**"

### Question 5: "What happens when a client reaches out after hours?"

> *"If someone WhatsApps you at 10pm wanting to book — what happens?"*

**What to listen for:**
- They miss it and respond the next morning → client booked elsewhere
- They see it but can't respond because they're asleep → same result
- They never see it because it gets buried in WhatsApp

**Note:** Every missed after-hours enquiry = one lost booking = R550–R3,500 lost.

### Question 6: "Have you ever had any problems with payments or fraud?"

> *"Have you ever had a client say they paid but you didn't receive it? Or any suspicious transactions?"*

**RED FLAG:** If fraud has happened and they have no system to verify payments — this is an immediate pain point and a reason to move fast.

### Question 7: "What would you do if you had 3 more hours per day — uninterrupted?"

> *"If tomorrow you woke up and had 3 extra hours every day — what would you do with that time?"*

**What to listen for:**
- More sessions = more revenue
- Content creation (TikTok growth)
- Family/personal time
- Strategic planning
- Developing new treatments

**Note:** Our system literally gives them 3+ hours per day back. This is not a soft benefit — it's a hard output.

---

## Section 5: Vision Extraction (28–38 minutes)

**Goal:** Let them describe the future they want. Anchor your solution to their vision.

### Question 8: "If you could wave a magic wand — what does IGH look like in 12 months?"

> *"Forget constraints for a second. If money and time were no object — what does success look like for IGH? How many clients per week? What does your day look like? What are you not doing anymore?"*

**What to listen for:**
- Specific revenue targets
- Number of locations
- Whether they want to be personally doing sessions or running a business
- Whether they want to expand into products
- Whether they want to travel or have more personal freedom

**Follow-up:** *"What is the single biggest thing standing between where you are now and that vision?"*

**This answer = the core pain point. This is what you solve.**

### Question 9: "Have you seen any competitor or wellness clinic that does things the way you wish things worked?"

> *"Have you been to another business — not necessarily in wellness — where the booking, payment, and communication just felt effortless? What did they do well?"*

**What to listen for:**
- Specific features (online booking, automatic reminders, easy payment)
- The emotional feeling ("it just felt professional")
- Whether they've tried any tools before and abandoned them (and why)

---

## Section 6: Close and Demo (38–43 minutes)

**Goal:** Show them exactly what their solution looks like. Create the emotional "I want that" moment.

### The Demo
> "Based on everything you've told me, I want to show you something. This is a demo we built for IGH — it shows exactly what your clinic would look like when we apply our system."

**Open:** https://zqjy41whi2rt.space.mcode.io

**Walk through:**
1. Show the hero — the beautiful, mobile-first website
2. Tap "Book Now" — show Cal.com booking flow
3. Show the WhatsApp AI response (simulate a 10pm enquiry)
4. Show the Huly CRM dashboard (contact, booking history, notes)
5. Show the Paystack payment confirmation screen

**Script while demoing:**
> "This is what your clinic looks like right now — when someone's searching for you online at 11pm from Polokwane. Right now they find a broken website and leave. With this system — they book, pay, and confirm — all in 60 seconds. And you wake up to a full day of appointments."

### Question 10: "Does that solve the problem you described earlier?"

**This is the most important question on the call.** If they say yes — you have a qualified client. If they say no — you have a discovery gap. Ask what it doesn't solve.

---

## Section 7: Next Steps (43–45 minutes)

### If qualified (they said yes to the demo solving their problem):
> "Great. Based on today's call, I'm going to put together a detailed proposal specifically for IGH — tailored to your 4 locations, your services, and the exact workflows you described. I'll send that within 24 hours.
>
> Before I do — is there anything I haven't asked that you think I should know about IGH?"

> "And one last thing — what would make you feel confident that this team can actually deliver what they're promising?"

**Their answer = your objection prevention for the proposal close.**

**Confirm:**
- [ ] Send proposal within 24 hours
- [ ] Schedule proposal walk-through call
- [ ] Send demo link to client immediately

### If not yet qualified:
> "I appreciate your honesty. Based on what you've told me, I don't think our system is the right fit right now — and I'd rather be upfront about that than waste your time. If things change, I'm here."

## Discovery Call Scoring Rubric

Use this after the call to score how ready the client is:

| Score | Label | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 9–10 | Ready to buy | They described specific pain, saw the demo, said "yes that's it" |
| 7–8 | Warm — needs proposal | Good discovery, demo resonated, minor objections |
| 5–6 | Interested — needs nurturing | Vision is clear, timing isn't right, send more content |
| 3–4 | Exploratory | Vague pain, not sure what they want, long sales cycle |
| 1–2 | Not a fit | Wrong industry, no budget, no decision-making authority |

**Rule:** Only send a formal proposal to clients scoring 7 or above. Everyone else gets a "continuing conversation" email.

## Common Objections and Responses

### "I don't have time to implement a new system."
> "That's exactly why our system works — you don't implement it. We do the whole build. You just get a login and a training session. We handle the website, the booking system, the payments, the WhatsApp AI, and the CRM. You're on the call for 1 hour. That's your time commitment."

### "My clients prefer WhatsApp — they won't use a website."
> "Your WhatsApp is actually the centre of our system. The AI assistant runs inside WhatsApp — your clients never have to open a website. The website is for NEW clients who find you on Google or TikTok. Your existing clients stay in WhatsApp. But now the WhatsApp is automated."

### "I tried something like this before and it didn't work."
> "What specifically didn't work? [Listen.] What we're offering is fundamentally different — we build a custom system for your specific clinic, your specific services, your specific client journey. Not a generic booking app. And we stay with you — monthly retainer means we're monitoring and optimising it every single month."

### "I need to think about it / run it past my husband / wait until next month."
> "Totally — big decisions deserve thought. What specifically are you thinking through? [Listen.] The reason I ask is — based on what you described today, every week we don't implement costs you approximately R___ in missed bookings. So the thinking has a real cost attached. Can we at least schedule the proposal walk-through call now so you have something concrete to think through?"

### "It's too expensive."
> "What would you expect to pay for a system that handles all your bookings, payments, client management, and WhatsApp automation — and gives you 3 hours a day back? [Listen.] Our setup is R62,000 and we recover that for you in week one. After that, you're in profit every single week. At R145,000/month in potential revenue — the question is really: can you afford NOT to do this?"
